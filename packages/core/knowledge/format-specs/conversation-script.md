# Conversation Script: how to write it

## What it is and when a teacher uses it
A short printable dialogue for pairs or small groups to read aloud or act out in an English or Filipino class. It gives learners model language for one everyday situation.

## Page setup
A4 portrait, 1-inch margins, Arial 12 (14 for Grades 1-3). One page; two at most.

## Parts, in order

| Part | What goes in it | Who supplies it | Length and style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Title and situation | Title and one line saying who is talking, where, why | Teacher gives topic; AI writes | 1-2 lines |
| Roles | Table: Role, Description | AI | 2-4 roles; short names |
| Dialogue | Table: Speaker, Line | AI | 8-16 turns; 1-2 sentences each |
| Vocabulary or expressions | Words and phrases from the dialogue with a short meaning | AI | 4-8 items |
| Practice prompts | 2-4 questions or tasks (retell, change a line, act with a new situation) | AI | One line each |

## How to fill each part
- **Situation.** Everyday and safe. Good: "Two classmates borrow a book from the school library." Bad: a situation about real people.
- **Roles.** Short, common Filipino names or labels (Ana, Ben, Librarian). Never real learners or teachers.
- **Dialogue.** Each turn answers the one before. Start with a greeting, state the need, answer, close politely. Use the target expressions at least twice.
- **Vocabulary.** Only words that appear in the dialogue; meaning in grade-level words.
- **Practice prompts.** Ask learners to use the language, not to guess the topic's wording.

## Writing rules
- One language per script (English or Filipino) as the teacher asks. Do not mix unless Taglish is requested.
- Reading level at or below the grade; short turns.
- Kind, classroom-safe content; no slang, no real people or places in a negative light.
- Every speaker speaks at least twice.

## Free checks code can run
- Number of speakers equals the request.
- Turn count within range (8 to 16).
- Every speaker has at least 2 lines.
- Every vocabulary item appears in the dialogue.
- Language check: no stretch of the other language.
- No bracket placeholders, no "TBD".

## AI must never write
- Real student, teacher or school names.
- A claim that the script is a DepEd material.
- Content about the wording of the topic itself instead of the situation.

## Sample (fictional, for style only)
**Title:** Borrowing a Book (Grade 4, English)
**Situation:** Ana asks the librarian to borrow a storybook.

| Role | Description |
|---|---|
| Ana | A Grade 4 learner who likes stories |
| Librarian | Helps learners find books |

| Speaker | Line |
|---|---|
| Ana | Good morning, Ma'am. May I borrow a book, please? |
| Librarian | Good morning, Ana. What kind of book would you like? |
| Ana | I would like a story about animals. |
| Librarian | Here is one about a clever crow. Please return it in one week. |
| Ana | Thank you, Ma'am. I will take good care of it. |
| Librarian | You are welcome. Enjoy reading! |

**Vocabulary:** borrow = take a book for a short time; return = give it back; take good care = keep it safe.
**Practice:** (1) Act out the dialogue with a partner. (2) Change the book and the day to return it. (3) Tell what Ana promised.
