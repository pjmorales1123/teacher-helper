# ELO Lesson Plan and Slide Outline: how to write it

## What it is and when a teacher uses it
It is a short ILAW-style plan for one lesson, plus a slide outline the teacher can turn into a deck. A teacher uses it when the lesson will be taught with slides on a screen or printed. Each step in the Flow has its own slide.

## Page setup
- A4, Arial 12, 1-inch margins for the plan.
- Part 1 is the plan. Part 2 starts on a new page with the slide outline as a table.
- Slides (if exported): 16:9, one idea per slide, text at least 24 pt.

## Parts, in order
| Part | What goes in it | Who supplies it | Length and style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Header | School, teacher, subject, grade, date, minutes | Profile, teacher | Facts only |
| Learning Competency | Competency and standards | Curriculum picker | Copied word for word |
| Learning Objectives | Cognitive, affective, psychomotor | AI | 3 short lines |
| Pre-Lesson | Hook or review | AI | 5 minutes |
| Flow | Numbered steps with titles and minutes | AI | 3 to 5 steps |
| Learning Resources | Materials, plus a backup | AI | A short list |
| Formative Assessment | Check of learning | AI | 2 to 4 sentences |
| Ways Forward | Extended learning idea | AI | One sentence |
| Reflections | Teacher notes after teaching | Teacher | Left blank |
| Slide outline | Slide number, title, on-slide text, speaker note, source Flow step | AI | One row per slide |

## How to fill each part
### Header and Learning Competency
Facts from the profile and picker. Never reworded.

### Learning Objectives
Three objectives. Start each with an action verb a teacher can see.
- Pattern: "Name the four stages of the water cycle (Cognitive)."
- Bad: "Learn about water." Not observable.

### Pre-Lesson
- Pattern: "Show a photo of a puddle drying in the sun. Ask: Where did the water go?"
- Bad: "Review." No action.

### Flow
Each step has a title, minutes and what the teacher and learners do. The steps add up to the lesson minutes. The last step is a 5-minute wrap-up.
- Pattern: "Explain (10 min): The teacher shows the water cycle diagram and names each stage. Learners point to each stage."
- Bad: "Discussion." No title, no minutes, no action.

### Learning Resources
Real, common items with one backup, such as chalk and board in place of a projector.

### Formative Assessment
A task that matches the cognitive objective, plus one way to ask for help.

### Slide outline
One slide per Flow step, in the same order. A title slide comes first. A closing slide may follow the last step.
- On-slide text: 3 short bullets at most, or one question or one picture idea.
- Speaker note: 1 to 2 sentences for the teacher.
- Good: "Slide 3 - Explain: Four stages. Evaporation, condensation, precipitation, collection."
- Bad: a whole paragraph copied onto the slide. Learners cannot read it.

## Writing rules
- One language, English or Filipino, as the teacher chooses.
- Short sentences. Plain words. Grade-level reading.
- Present tense for the teacher's actions.
- Slide text is shorter than the plan text. No sentence on a slide is over 12 words.
- No claims about the school or learners.
- Avoid "understand", "know", "appreciate" in objectives.

## Free checks code can run
- Objectives: exactly 3.
- Flow minutes add up to the lesson minutes. Pre-Lesson is 5 and wrap-up is 5.
- Slide count equals Flow steps plus the title slide (plus the closing slide if present).
- Each slide row has a title and a source step number.
- On-slide text is at most 3 bullets and each bullet is at most 12 words.
- Competency text equals the picker text.
- No placeholder text.

## AI must never write
- Names, dates, signatories, school facts.
- Competency text or standards.
- The teacher's Reflections.
- Questions about the wording of the topic itself.
- Facts in slides that are not in the lesson (no invented statistics).

## Sample (fictional, for style only)
Grade 5 Science, 45 minutes. School and teacher are placeholders.

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Header | [School Name], [Teacher Name], Science, Grade 5, [Date], 45 min |
| Learning Competency | [Copied from the curriculum picker] |
| Objectives | 1. Name the four stages of the water cycle (Cognitive) <br> 2. Listen to a partner's explanation (Affective) <br> 3. Draw and label the water cycle (Psychomotor) |
| Pre-Lesson (5 min) | The teacher shows a photo of a wet pavement drying in the sun and asks, "Where did the water go?" |
| Flow | 1. **Explain (10 min):** The teacher shows a diagram and names evaporation, condensation, precipitation and collection. <br> 2. **Act It Out (10 min):** Groups act out one stage each and the class guesses it. <br> 3. **Draw and Label (15 min):** Learners draw the cycle and label four stages. <br> 4. **Wrap-up (5 min):** Learners tell a partner the order of the stages. |
| Learning Resources | Diagram on manila paper, paper, crayons. Backup: draw the diagram on the board. |
| Formative Assessment | Learners put four stage cards in order. A learner who is stuck asks a partner first, then the teacher. |
| Ways Forward | Watch for clouds and puddles at home and tell the class one thing you saw. |
| Reflections | [Teacher to write after the lesson] |

| Slide | Title | On-slide text | Speaker note | Flow step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Water Cycle | Grade 5 Science | Greet the class and state the goal. | Title |
| 2 | Where Did the Water Go? | Photo of a drying pavement. Question: Where did the water go? | Take two or three answers. | Pre-Lesson |
| 3 | Four Stages | Evaporation. Condensation. Precipitation. Collection. | Point to each stage on the diagram. | 1 Explain |

## Open points
- "ELO" has no official template. The owner should confirm that an ILAW-style plan is the right base.
- Whether slides should be 16:9 .pptx only or also a printable handout.
- Maximum slide text (3 bullets, 12 words) is an app rule, not a DepEd rule.
