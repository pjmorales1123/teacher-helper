# Read-aloud Passage: how to write it

## What it is and when a teacher uses it
A short passage the teacher reads aloud or plays as audio, with comprehension questions the learners answer. It supports listening and reading practice at the learners' grade.

## Page setup
A4 portrait, 1-inch margins. Passage font by grade (Phil-IRI copy): Comic Sans 18 for K-1, 16 for Grade 2, 14 for Grade 3, 12 for Grades 4-7. Use Arial or the school font when Comic Sans is not available.

## Parts, in order

| Part | What goes in it | Who supplies it | Length and style |
|---|---|---|---|
| Header | Title, grade, language | Teacher; AI suggests title | 1-2 lines |
| Passage | The reading text with the word count shown | AI | Narrative for Grades 2-4, expository for 5-7 |
| Comprehension questions | Numbered questions on the passage | AI | 5-8 questions (about 5 for K-1, 6 for Grades 2-3, 7 for Grades 4-5, 8 for Grades 6-7, unverified) |
| Answer key | Short expected answer per question | AI | Teacher copy only |

## How to fill each part
- **Passage.** One clear idea, a beginning, middle and end for stories; a topic sentence and facts for informative texts. Everyday Filipino settings. Proposed length (unverified): Grade 1 about 40 words, 2 about 60, 3 about 80, 4 about 100, 5 about 120, 6 about 140, 7 about 150.
- **Questions.** Mix literal (answer is stated), inferential (answer must be worked out) and critical (opinion with a reason). Ask about the passage, never about its wording or title.
- **Key.** One short expected answer; accept reasonable alternatives for inferential and critical items.

## Writing rules
- Words and sentence length for the grade; no unexplained rare words.
- Facts must be correct for informative passages.
- No names of real people, schools or learners.
- One language per passage.

## Free checks code can run
- Word count inside the grade range.
- Question count matches the grade rule.
- Key has one answer per question.
- Passage type matches the grade (narrative vs expository).
- At least one literal and one inferential question.
- Each question can be answered from the passage alone.

## AI must never write
- Reading levels, scores or Phil-IRI classifications for any learner.
- Names of real learners or teachers.
- Questions that need outside knowledge.

## Sample (fictional, for style only)
**Header:** The Rainy Day Market (Grade 3, English). 78 words.

**Passage:** It rained hard on Saturday. Mang Ben still opened his fruit stall in the market. He covered the mangoes with a big plastic sheet. Soon, a girl named Lia came with her mother. "May I help you, Mang Ben?" she asked. She held the sheet while he stacked more fruit. When the rain stopped, many people came to buy. Mang Ben gave Lia a ripe mango. "Thank you for helping," he said. Lia smiled.

**Questions**
1. What did Mang Ben cover with a plastic sheet?
2. Who came to the stall with her mother?
3. Why did Mang Ben give Lia a mango?
4. How do you think Lia felt at the end? Why?
5. What would you do to help someone on a rainy day?
6. What happened when the rain stopped?

**Key (teacher copy):** 1 the mangoes; 2 Lia; 3 she helped him; 4 happy, because she was thanked (accept similar); 5 any kind, polite answer; 6 many people came to buy.
