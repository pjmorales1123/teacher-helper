# Outline (DO 16, s. 2017, Research Management Guidelines)
Completed report: Title Page, Abstract, Acknowledgment, Context and Rationale, Innovation/Intervention/Strategy,
Action Research Questions, Action Research Methods (participants and/or other sources of data, data gathering
methods, data analysis plan), Discussion of Results and Reflection, Action Research Work Plan and Timelines,
Cost Estimates, Plans for Dissemination and Utilization, References, Appendices.
Proposal: Context and Rationale, Action Research Questions, Proposed Innovation/Intervention/Strategy, Action
Research Methods, Work Plan and Timelines, Cost Estimates, Plans for Dissemination and Advocacy, References.
Keep the division's template if it differs.

# What goes in each part
- Title: the intervention, the skill/outcome, the participants, the school and SY (e.g. "Using Reading Buddies to
  Improve Oral Reading Fluency of Grade 3 Learners of San Isidro ES, SY 2026-2027").
- Context and Rationale: the problem in this class with the teacher's own baseline data, why it matters, and
  what the literature says (real citations only).
- Questions: specific and answerable with the data planned (e.g. pre/post means, difference, perceptions).
- Methods: participants (number, how chosen, consent/assent of parents and learners), instruments (validated or
  adapted, with source), procedure by week, analysis matched to each question (mean, SD, paired t-test or
  Wilcoxon for small groups, thematic analysis for interviews).
- Results: tables with "Table 1." titles above, then interpretation; reflection on what worked and limits.
  Never fabricate results: use [DATA: pre-test mean] placeholders until the teacher gives numbers.
- Work plan: activities, timeline, persons involved. Cost estimates: items, totals, source of funds.
- Abstract: one paragraph, about 150-250 words: problem, intervention, participants, method, key result, conclusion.

# Citations
- APA 7th edition unless the division says otherwise. In-text (Author, Year); reference list alphabetical,
  hanging indent, DOI as https://doi.org/... when there is one.
- Only cite works you found in the teacher's files or through web search and actually opened; otherwise write
  [CITATION NEEDED: claim]. Name the source of each new citation in your reply so the teacher can check it.

# How to work on a research paper
1. Identify whether it is a proposal or a completed report and keep the matching outline.
2. Fill parts in order; keep the teacher's data; use placeholders for missing data and citations.
3. Keep the questions, methods, results and conclusions aligned (each question has a method and a result).
4. Check APA format, table/figure numbering, and that no learner is identifiable.
