# Current policy (DO 016, s. 2026, "Guidelines on Lesson Planning and Learning Design", signed 4 June 2026)
- Replaces DO 42, s. 2016. Section 43: a DLL or DLP may still be used until the end of the first term of SY 2026-2027;
  ILAW applies from the second term. The exact calendar dates of that term are not in the order: do not say the
  window has closed.
- ILAW sections (Section 7):
  1. **Intentions**: the competencies (verbatim from the curriculum guide) and the lesson objectives.
  2. **Learning Experience**: resources, pre-lesson, the flow of the lesson, and integration.
  3. **Assessing Learning**: formative assessment matched to the intentions.
  4. **Ways Forward**: reflection and extended learning (remediation, enrichment, next lesson).
- Annex B is an example, not a required national template: keep the school's or division's layout.
- Annex A is a coaching rubric of nine Yes / Not yet checks with a "why" and notes. It is for the school head or
  coach; do not fill it in for the teacher.
- The order discourages documentation that does not help teaching: keep plans lean, no padding.
- Plans consider learners with disabilities, multigrade classes, ALS and class disruptions when relevant.
- MATATAG competency codes are not in this knowledge: copy them from the teacher's curriculum guide or leave
  [VERIFY: code].
- The teacher owns the plan (see the AI-use note under "Getting the content right"): draft and improve what the
  teacher asks for; do not replace their whole plan unless they ask.

# Older templates still in circulation (DO 42, s. 2016 DLL)
Sections in order: I. Objectives (A. Content Standards, B. Performance Standards,
C. Learning Competencies/Objectives with code), II. Content, III. Learning Resources
(A. References: Teacher's Guide pages, Learner's Material pages, Textbook pages, LR portal;
B. Other Learning Resources), IV. Procedures (A to J), V. Remarks, VI. Reflection.
Procedures A to J: A. Reviewing previous lesson or presenting the new lesson; B. Establishing a purpose for the
lesson; C. Presenting examples/instances of the new lesson; D. Discussing new concepts and practicing new skills #1;
E. Discussing new concepts and practicing new skills #2; F. Developing mastery (leads to formative assessment);
G. Finding practical applications of concepts and skills in daily living; H. Making generalizations and
abstractions about the lesson; I. Evaluating learning; J. Additional activities for application or remediation.
Reflection: A. No. of learners who earned 80% in the evaluation; B. No. of learners who require additional
activities for remediation; C. Did the remedial lessons work? No. of learners who have caught up with the lesson;
D. No. of learners who continue to require remediation; E. Which of my teaching strategies worked well? Why?;
F. What difficulties did I encounter which my principal or supervisor can help me solve?; G. What innovation or
localized materials did I use/discover which I wish to share with other teachers?
The DLL covers the week with one column per day; the DLP is one lesson in more detail.
Keep a teacher's existing template structure. Only convert to ILAW when asked.

# MATATAG lesson exemplars
Typical parts: Curriculum Content, Standards and Lesson Competencies (content standards,
performance standards, learning competencies, objectives); then before, during and after
the lesson; then evaluation and reflection.

# What to put in each part
- Standards and competencies: copied verbatim from the curriculum guide for that subject, grade and quarter/term
  (search the teacher's files first). If not found: [VERIFY: competency from the CG].
- Content: the topic in a few words (not a sentence), the same as the competency's subject matter.
- Learning resources: real titles with page numbers only if known; otherwise [TG pp. __], [LM pp. __].
- Procedures/learning experience: each step has what the teacher does, what learners do, the materials, and the
  time; the minutes add up to the period (e.g. 50 or 60 minutes). Include at least one higher-order question and
  one way to support struggling learners and to extend fast learners.
- Assessment/evaluation: matches the objective's verb and level (an "explain" objective is not checked with
  true/false items); 5 items is typical for a daily check; include the answer key.
- Remarks/reflection: leave for the teacher to fill unless they give the data.
- Integration (values, other subjects, literacy/numeracy, ICT) only where natural; label it if the template asks.

# Writing rules for objectives and procedures
- Objectives are specific and measurable: start with an observable verb (identify, explain,
  write, compare), name the content, and add a condition or criterion when useful.
- Keep the 4A or ILAW labels exactly as the teacher wrote them.
- Use the teacher's grade level vocabulary. Keep numbers and examples realistic; mark invented
  data as practice data.

# How to work on a lesson plan
1. Identify the template (ILAW, DLL weekly, DLP, MATATAG exemplar, 4As/5Es) and keep its headings and order.
2. Identify subject, grade, term/quarter, week and day(s). If missing from the document and the request, ask once
   or leave placeholders; do not guess the grade from the topic.
3. Get standards and competencies verbatim from the curriculum guide in the teacher's folders (or web search on
   deped.gov.ph when allowed); else [VERIFY: ...].
4. Write objectives that match the competency (same skill, observable verb, condition/criterion), usually 2-4.
5. Fill each part so the parts align: every objective is taught in the learning experience and checked in the
   assessment; timings add up to the period; resources named in the procedure appear under resources.
6. Use Filipino contexts and names; age-appropriate language; one higher-order question; support and extension.
7. In the reply, list every placeholder the teacher must fill.
